# Bindery patch — October 17, 2026 (13): opponents with their own reasons, one point of view, a plan check
The bundle is now index-27923723.js.
1. Stories bent the world around the main character. In a Flash story the Joker scattered laughing-gas grenades for no reason, a car outran the Flash because the scene needed him far away, and everyone besides the hero acted only to set up the hero's lesson. The planner now plans every opponent as good at what they do, with a plan that makes sense from where they stand and that they would carry out even if the hero never showed up. Nobody gets weaker, slower or less clever than they've been shown to be just so a scene can happen; if a rule bends, the story shows why. Fights and arguments don't all end in agreement.
2. A plan check runs after the outline and before any prose. It reads the plan as a reader would and re-plans only the chapters where someone is suddenly weaker than they are, an opponent acts with no reason of their own, something happens without a cause, a lesson is handed over or said out loud, or a clash ends in everyone agreeing. A failed check is skipped; it never stops the book.
3. One point of view unless the brief asks for more. The writer used to get every character's wants, knowledge and relationships, and wrote them all, so the narrator explained side characters' insides. Now, unless the brief, charter or style guide asks for an omniscient narrator or multiple perspectives, the writer gets only the chapter's point-of-view character's inner life; everyone else comes as where they are, how they are and what they've been called. The chapter checker still sees everything.
4. Under pressure, less thinking. The writer is told: when someone is in danger, fighting or rushing, stay with what they see, hear and do; thoughts come in fragments if at all. Don't explain why anyone does what they do; the reader works it out from what they did.
5. Characters from an existing film, book, comic or game keep their powers and limits, beliefs and way of talking unless the brief changes them. Research mode now also covers the cast step, so they can be looked up.

# Bindery patch — September 29, 2026 (10): the skeleton outline no longer fails on a short reply
The bundle is now index-1e5f5d0c.js.
1. Long books failed at "Outline · skeleton: Model returned JSON without the required structure." The skeleton was accepted only with a "seeds" list covering 80% of the chapters. A reply cut off by the output limit, or one that filed the list under "chapters" or "outline" or one level down, failed both attempts.
2. The list is now found under "seeds", "chapters", "outline", "skeleton", "book" or "plan", one level down, or as a bare array. Items may be plain strings, and "summary" or "logline" count as the gist.

# Bindery patch — September 29, 2026 (8): the planner understands, the writer lives it
The bundle is now index-fb9da8a2.js.
The ground was handed to the writer, the editor and the record-keeper as a plan to carry out, and the record measured each character's grip after every chapter. Sonnet followed it: the Mask's joking was filed as a stuck grip, pressed for five chapters and let go of, with a dog out of the ground's own examples reading everyone's feelings. George Saunders (a Nyingma practitioner) calls this an overdetermined story: the writer saying what it planned to say whatever the story answers.
1. The ground goes to the planner only (cast and outline), as its understanding. The planner is told none of its words or ideas go into the notes.
2. Character notes are what a novelist jots down for themselves: what it is, what they're like, what they want, what they can and can't do, how they talk, how they stand with the others. The grip, fears, stuck-style, patterns and conscience fields are gone, and so is the example in the template.
3. "Where the tone comes from" is now "The world, and what the book is after": what the world is like to live in, what its people take for granted and fear, how they treat what they don't understand (as it is in its source if it has one), and what the author wants a reader to feel.
4. The writer gets the brief first, then the requirements, style, world, characters and the book so far, and one line on how to write: from inside the people living each scene, in the world as it is.
5. The record keeps facts only: where each person is, their condition, what they know and want, who they're allied with, what they've been called on the page. Grip, holding, misreads, patterns, let-go and "how they changed" are no longer recorded or sent on.
6. The editor reads as the reader. The ground checklist and the "states the meaning outright" check are gone. In their place: up to four places where it stopped believing or stopped caring, quoted, with what happened in it there. These can send a chapter back, within the rewrite limit.
Books planned before this patch keep their old notes, but the writer sees only the plain fields. Re-running the cast step writes new ones.

# Bindery patch — September 29, 2026 (6): any being that is aware
The bundle is now index-dee44fc9.js.

1. The ground text is now "How anything that is aware works". It covers people, animals, swarms, gods, ghosts and machines that know themselves. Each kind of being grips through what it has, and lives in the world its senses and needs make, at its own scale of time. It is written from inside that world, never as a human in a costume. Things that aren't aware don't grip; they are how the world appears to the beings that do.
2. Character notes gain "what it is and how it perceives": its senses, what its world is made of, its scale of time. "How they talk" is now "how it talks or communicates" (voice, body, sound, signal).
3. The editor also flags a non-human written as a human in a costume.
